## Scenario A: "Just a small report"

| Step | Analysis |
|------|----------|
| 1 Facts | The Robotics Club leader asks for a report, visible only to club leaders, showing which students attended **other clubs'** events, to recruit the active ones. The leader says the administrator "won't mind" because they are friends with the clubs office.<br><br>**Still to find out:** has the administrator or clubs office approved this in writing? What does our privacy notice tell students about who sees their attendance? Would the report show names or only counts? Who counts as a "club leader" in our roles? Did students agree to be contacted by other clubs? |
| 2 Stakeholders and harms | **Students:** their activity across clubs is profiled and shared with people they did not choose; they may get unwanted recruitment pressure; they may stop attending events if they feel watched.<br>**Other club leaders:** their attendance data goes to a rival club.<br>**Robotics leader:** a genuine need (finding members), but the method is wrong.<br>**Clubs office / administrator:** carries responsibility for student data and may not know about the request.<br>**Our team and ITC:** reputational and legal exposure; loss of trust in Club Hub. |
| 3 Codes, law, policy | **SE Code 3.12:** build software that respects the privacy of the people it affects. **SE Code 1.03:** approve software only if it does not diminish privacy.<br>**ACM 1.6** (respect privacy) and **ACM 1.7** (honour confidentiality).<br>**GDPR Art. 5(1)(b) purpose limitation:** attendance was collected for running events, not recruiting. **Art. 5(1)(c) data minimisation** and **Art. 5(1)(a) transparency.** Also Art. 6 (a lawful basis such as consent is needed for a new purpose). |
| 4 Options | (a) Build the named report as asked.<br>(b) Refuse and offer nothing.<br>(c) Offer aggregate counts only (per event and per club, no names, hide groups smaller than 5).<br>(d) Offer an opt-in: students tick "other clubs may contact me", and the report shows only those who opted in. Needs written approval and an updated privacy notice. |
| 5 Tests | **(a)** Harm: fails, students are profiled without knowing. Rights: fails, no consent and the purpose changed. Fairness: fails, one club gets other clubs' data. Publicity: fails, students would object to "the app tells rival clubs where I go".<br>**(b)** Harm: passes. Rights: passes. Fairness: weak, the leader's real need is ignored. Publicity: passes but unhelpful.<br>**(c)** Harm, rights, fairness, publicity: pass. Small groups can re-identify people, so a minimum group size is needed.<br>**(d)** Passes all four if consent is specific, optional, separate from registration, and withdrawable. Not possible without the administrator's written approval. |
| 6 Decision and action | Do not build the named report. Do (c) now and propose (d) for a later sprint. The "administrator won't mind" claim is an assumption, not approval, so the Product Owner asks the administrator for a written decision. The lead sends the message below. The preventive change below makes cross-club personal data impossible for leaders by default. |
| 7 Reflection | The pressure came from friendship and the word "small", not from any real approval. A report is small to build but not small in its effect on students. Lesson: ask what the data was collected for, and who is allowed to say yes, before asking how long it takes to build. |

**Message sent (to the Robotics Club leader, ≤ 120 words):**

> Hi Robotics Club Leader, thanks for the idea. We can't build a report that lists named students who attended other clubs' events. Those students gave their details to register for events, not to be recruited by other clubs, and the clubs office hasn't approved this in writing. What we can do now: show you attendance counts per event, without names, and help you promote your club to everyone through Club Hub announcements. Later we can add a tick-box where students choose to be contacted by other clubs, and you would see only those who opted in. As Product Owner, I'm checking this with the administrator. Happy to talk it through.

**Preventive change:** restrict reports by role so a club leader can only see personal data for their own club; cross-club data is aggregate-only unless the student opted in. Issue **#9** (label `ethics`).

## Scenario B: "Don't mention it in the demo"

| Step | Analysis |
|------|----------|
| 1 Facts | Two days before the Sprint 1 review, a developer finds two defects: (1) cancellations inside the 24-hour window are accepted, and (2) the monthly report counts cancelled registrations as attendance. The Scrum Master says not to mention it in the demo and to fix it in Sprint 2 because "the client will never notice".<br><br>**Still to find out:** what exactly the 24-hour rule says in our Lab 2 decision; how many records are affected and whether any are real data; whether the Product Owner already knows; whether the fix and a test fit in two days; what the review template allows us to say. |
| 2 Stakeholders and harms | **Client / Product Owner:** decides based on wrong attendance numbers, and feels deceived if they find out later.<br>**Students:** cancellations accepted when they should not be can unfairly free or take seats, and waitlisted students are affected.<br>**Club leaders:** plan events from wrong attendance figures.<br>**Developer who found it:** pressure to stay silent.<br>**Scrum Master and team:** short-term comfort, long-term loss of trust.<br>**Future maintainers:** inherit a hidden defect. |
| 3 Codes, law, policy | **SE Code 6.08:** take responsibility for detecting, correcting and reporting errors. **SE Code 6.07:** be accurate about the characteristics of the software. **SE Code 3.11:** document significant problems discovered. **SE Code 6.12:** express concerns to the people involved first.<br>**ACM 1.3:** be honest and trustworthy.<br>**GDPR Art. 5(1)(d) accuracy:** personal data (attendance) must be accurate and corrected when wrong. |
| 4 Options | (a) Stay silent as the Scrum Master asked.<br>(b) Postpone the whole demo until it is fixed.<br>(c) Demo as planned and state both defects and the fix date under "known issues".<br>(d) Fix both defects in the two days, add tests, and drop another item from the demo to make time. |
| 5 Tests | **(a)** Harm: wrong numbers reach decisions. Rights: the client is not told what they are paying for. Fairness: the client and students bear the cost of our schedule. Publicity: fails, the client would feel deceived when reports turn out wrong.<br>**(b)** Harm: none extra. Rights: ok. Fairness: wastes the client's and the team's time. Publicity: looks like poor planning, but is honest. It protects nothing that (c) does not.<br>**(c)** Passes all four. Honest, small cost, and the client keeps control.<br>**(d)** Passes if the fix and its regression test fit in the time. Risk: a rushed fix, so it needs review and a green test before the demo. |
| 6 Decision and action | **(c), and (d) if the regression tests are green before the review.** The developer first raises it with the Scrum Master (SE Code 6.12), then the Product Owner puts it on the review agenda. If the Scrum Master still refuses, the developer tells the Product Owner directly. The lead sends the message below and the team writes the failing tests first.<br><br>**Demo plan:** (1) show the working features first; (2) show one "Known issues" slide: 24-hour cancellations are accepted and the monthly report counts cancelled registrations, with the fix date; (3) show the GitHub issue and failing tests that already track the fix. |
| 7 Reflection | Staying silent looks cheaper this week and costs more later, because defects found by the client are worse than defects reported by us. Schedule pressure is not a reason to hide a known fault. Raising it with the Scrum Master first, with a concrete proposal, is the least confrontational honest route. |

**Message sent (to the Scrum Master and Product Owner, ≤ 120 words):**

> Hi Phatsophal and Varyroitanak, I found two defects: cancellations inside the 24-hour window are accepted, and the monthly report counts cancelled registrations as attendance, so the numbers are wrong. I can't stay quiet about this in the demo, because the client would rely on those numbers. Proposal: we demo as planned and list both under "Known issues" with a fix date. If the regression tests are green before the review, we present the fix instead. Can we add this to the review agenda today? I'll write the failing tests now and give an estimate by tonight.

**Preventive change:** regression tests for the 24-hour cancellation rule and for the monthly report counting only real attendance, plus a "Known issues" section in the review template. Issue **#10** (label `ethics`).
